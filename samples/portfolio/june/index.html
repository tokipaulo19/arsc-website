A SIMPLE WAY OF WORKING

From a first hello
to something you keep.

  1. A conversation, first.

    We talk about the occasion, the setting and how you’d like the photographs to feel. I’ll explain the coverage, usage and practical details so you know what the collection includes.

  2. Room for the unexpected.

    We make a loose plan around light and timing, then leave room for real moments. Family group photographs can have their own short list; the rest of the day doesn’t need to feel like a production.

  3. A considered edit.

    The collection is edited for colour, rhythm and a sense of the whole story. You’d receive a private gallery with download instructions, print guidance and clearly agreed usage notes.

FROM THE NOTEBOOK / WHY I LOOK THIS WAY

The photograph between
the photographs.

The formal portrait matters. So does the hand on a shoulder just before it. I’m interested in both: the picture you imagined, and the moment you didn’t know you’d want to remember.

For the fictional garden story above, the starting point was the dinner table rather than the ceremony aisle. Warm light, borrowed chairs and the feeling of everyone leaning into the same conversation. The edit moves from a wider sense of place to the people inside it.

That’s also why I avoid prescribing matching outfits for portraits. A familiar jumper, a favourite colour or the shoes you always wear can say more than an elaborate styling plan. A photograph should hold something of your everyday life, not erase it.

BEYOND THE SCREEN

Made to be held.
Made to live with.

A small print.

A favourite photograph on the desk often does more than a thousand files in a folder. The imagined print collection begins with unframed fine-art paper prints in 5×7 and 8×10 inch sizes, with paper samples discussed before ordering.

A quiet album.

An album would follow the story rather than a fixed number of poses: the setting, the people, the transitions. A sample design would be reviewed before production, with dimensions, materials and final price agreed in writing.

Your files, safely kept.

For a real commission, download the high-resolution files promptly and keep a second copy somewhere separate. A gallery is a delivery method, not an indefinite archive. This demo does not host client galleries or store personal photographs.

BEFORE YOU ASK ME ALONG

A few answers.

We’re awkward in front of a camera. Is that a problem?

Not at all. The concept approach makes time for a walk, a conversation and simple prompts. There are moments for gentle direction, but you don’t need to arrive knowing how to pose.

Do you travel, and what if the weather changes?

Travel would be agreed in the quote, including transport and any overnight stay. For an outdoor portrait, we’d discuss a sheltered alternative or a new date. There is no actual calendar, travel offer or weather policy behind this fictional site.

Will you share our photographs publicly?

A real commission would discuss publication permission separately from image delivery. Personal photographs should not be treated as automatic marketing material. Every image on this page is generated, not a real client photograph.

Can we see more than one wedding story?

Yes—choose Weddings above to explore the two different fictional studies, then use the lightbox arrows or keyboard left/right keys. These show the proposed browsing experience, not full delivered galleries.

Are unedited files included?

The illustrative collections include edited photographs, not working files or outtakes. A real agreement would specify delivery format, resolution, licensing and any exceptions before the session.
