BEFORE YOU BUY / QUESTIONS WORTH ASKING

CHECK THE FIT.
NOT JUST THE PRICE.

MEMORY ISN’T UNIVERSAL.

Start with your motherboard’s exact model and supported memory generation. DDR4 and DDR5 desktop modules are not interchangeable. Check the board documentation, capacity limits and supported configurations; a advertised memory speed is not a promise for every system.

SPACE & POWER MATTER.

A graphics upgrade needs more than an available slot. Confirm case clearance, slot thickness, the power supply’s capacity and the required connectors. Cooling and cable routing also matter. The sample graphics card view lists its intended dimensions to show where those details belong.

KEEP YOUR FILES IN THE PLAN.

An SSD upgrade can mean a fresh installation or a migration, depending on the device and your goals. Discuss backups, encryption keys, drive access and software licences before work begins. Never assume a storage upgrade automatically includes successful data recovery.

General buying checks, not a compatibility assessment of your machine. Consult the actual manufacturers’ documentation before a purchase.

NO BLACK BOX / THE WORKSHOP PROCESS

YOU SHOULD KNOW
WHAT HAPPENS NEXT.

  1. CHECK IN.

    Record the device, accessories and reported symptoms together. Discuss backups and encryption before handing it over. Do not send passwords, recovery keys or confidential files through a website enquiry.

  2. ASSESS & EXPLAIN.

    Investigate within the agreed diagnostic scope. Explain the findings, repair options, parts availability and the limitations of the assessment, including when replacement may make more sense.

  3. APPROVE, THEN WORK.

    Get written approval for the agreed repair and price before ordering parts or doing extra work. A live service would provide realistic updates if the diagnosis or timeline changes.

  4. TEST & HAND OVER.

    Explain the completed work and the checks performed. Provide an itemised invoice, relevant support terms and practical next steps. The workshop demo below stops before any device is booked in.

WORKSHOP FAQ

STRAIGHT
ANSWERS.

Will the website diagnose my computer?

No. It previews a repair intake, not a diagnosis. Symptoms can have several causes, and no hardware is examined here. A real technician would assess your device within a clearly agreed scope.

Can you recover data from a failed drive?

This concept does not offer or promise data recovery. A real service would explain the risks, specialist referral options and consent requirements before attempting work. Never put recovery keys or passwords into the demo form.

Can you install parts I already own?

The imagined workshop would first review the exact models, condition, compatibility and intended work. A real quote would explain responsibility for customer-supplied parts and any testing limits before accepting them.

Are the catalogue specifications real?

No. Bench G8, DDR5 Kit, NV1 and Air Case are fictional workshop-label products designed to demonstrate detailed product pages. Their imagery and specifications cannot be used to choose or validate actual hardware.

What happens after I submit a repair preview?

A local dialog summarises the device and issue you chose. No email is sent, no job number is issued, no appointment is reserved, and none of your input is retained.
